🚀 ASTRA — Autonomous Space Resource Architecture

The Asteroid That Pays for Its Own Mining

STAC WARS | PS4 | Mining the Asteroid Belt | IIT Mandi

ASTRA is a proposed modular and reusable space-resource supply network designed to extract water from suitable water-bearing asteroids and deliver it to customers in space.

Instead of treating asteroid mining as a one-time mission, ASTRA proposes a reusable spacecraft carrier with replaceable mining modules, solar-thermal extraction, water purification, and in-space delivery.

Our vision: Turn asteroid mining into a staged, reusable, and economically evaluated space-resource business.

---

🌌 1. The Problem

Future space exploration and infrastructure may require reliable access to resources such as water. Transporting all supplies from Earth can be costly, while extracting resources from asteroids presents significant engineering and economic challenges.

Key challenges include:

- High transportation costs: Launching and transporting equipment from Earth requires substantial investment.
- Resource uncertainty: The availability and accessibility of water on a target asteroid must be established.
- Difficult extraction: Weak gravity complicates anchoring, material handling, and autonomous operations.
- Mission failures: Equipment failures can cause costly delays or jeopardize an entire mission.
- Unproven economics: Resource extraction must compete with alternative supply routes at a commercially viable delivered cost.

ASTRA addresses these challenges through modular spacecraft design, prospecting before extraction, reusable infrastructure, and evidence-based commercialization.

💡 2. Our Proposed Solution

ASTRA separates the reusable spacecraft carrier from the replaceable mining hardware.

This modular architecture is intended to allow a failed or worn mining pod to be isolated and replaced without necessarily sacrificing the entire carrier.

Core features

- Reusable carrier: Supports spacecraft navigation, communications, autonomy, and resource-processing operations.
- Replaceable mining pods: Allow modular maintenance and mission-specific equipment replacement.
- Solar-thermal extraction: Concentrates sunlight to heat suitable water-bearing asteroid material.
- Water capture and purification: Contains vapour, condenses it, purifies the collected water, and prepares it for storage.
- In-space delivery: Supplies water to compatible customers or storage depots.
- Fault management: Detects faults, isolates affected systems, and transitions to safe mode, backup systems, repair, or replacement where feasible.

Hydrogen–oxygen propellant production is proposed as a later upgrade, after the initial water-extraction and delivery system is demonstrated.

Mission stages

1. Prospect: Map composition, accessible water, surface hazards, rotation, and transfer requirements.
2. Extract: Concentrate sunlight on suitable water-bearing material.
3. Capture: Contain, condense, and collect the released vapour.
4. Purify: Process and test the collected water.
5. Deliver: Transport the stored resource to a compatible customer or depot.
6. Reuse: Inspect the carrier, replace worn modules, and prepare for another mission.

Critical engineering challenges: Weak-gravity anchoring, water accessibility, heat management, vapour containment, and autonomous operation.

💰 5. Cost-Optimisation Strategy

ASTRA aims to reduce the cost of space-resource delivery through design choices rather than assuming unverified cost savings.

Cost pressure| Proposed design response| What must be validated
Transportation| Lightweight carrier, efficient transfers, reusable hardware, and in-space delivery| Mass estimates, transfer simulations, and delivered-cost analysis
Extraction| Solar-thermal processing and replaceable equipment| Asteroid composition, operating environment, and extraction tests
Failure and downtime| Prospecting, fault isolation, redundant critical electronics, and replaceable modules| Reliability, recovery, and repair assumptions

Design principles

- Reusable missions instead of one-off spacecraft.
- Replaceable modules instead of an entirely custom processing plant for every mission.
- Prospecting before mining to reduce resource uncertainty.
- In-space resource delivery instead of automatically returning all extracted material to Earth.

The cost advantage remains a design objective that requires testing, simulation, and economic validation.

🏢 6. Business Model

ASTRA is envisioned as a space-resource logistics business, not simply a company selling mined asteroid material.

Products and services

Phase 1 — Purified water

Recover, purify, store, and deliver water to customers in space.

Phase 2 — Hydrogen and oxygen propellant

Explore propellant production after water recovery, processing capabilities, and customer demand have been demonstrated.

Phase 3 — Shared storage and distribution

Develop shared resource-storage and distribution infrastructure when demand and economics justify it.

🧪 8. Validation and Risk Management

ASTRA is a proposed architecture, not a demonstrated commercial mining system. Its feasibility must be established through staged testing.

Technical validation

- Characterise candidate asteroids before committing to extraction.
- Establish a minimum resource-yield threshold.
- Ground-test water recovery, vapour containment, condensation, and purification.
- Demonstrate reliable autonomous operations and modular fault recovery.
- Conduct a small-scale flight demonstration before considering operational expansion.

Market validation

- Obtain credible customer commitments.
- Compare delivered-water costs with alternative supply routes.
- Verify demand before investing in shared storage or distribution infrastructure.

Key risks

Risk| Validation approach
Inaccessible water| Prospecting and resource characterisation
Low extraction efficiency| Ground testing and recovery measurements
High mission costs| Mass estimates, mission simulations, and delivered-cost analysis
Hardware failure| Fault isolation, redundancy, and replaceable modules
Insufficient demand| Customer commitments and competing-supply analysis

## 🔭 10. Future Scope

Potential future developments include:

- More detailed asteroid prospecting and accessible-water estimation.
- Improved thermal extraction and vapour-capture technologies.
- Autonomous spacecraft navigation and weak-gravity anchoring demonstrations.
- Improved purification, storage, and in-space transportation systems.
- Hydrogen–oxygen propellant production after the initial water system is validated.
- Shared orbital or lunar resource-storage and distribution infrastructure.
- Detailed mission simulations and sensitivity analysis of the economic model.

Each development should depend on demonstrated technical performance and a credible commercial case.

## 🎯 11. Project Objective

ASTRA aims to explore how asteroid mining could evolve from an expensive, one-off extraction mission into a modular and reusable space-resource supply network.

By prioritising water extraction, replaceable hardware, in-space delivery, and staged investment, the project proposes a pathway for evaluating the future feasibility of space-based resource logistics.

**Fund evidence first. Scale infrastructure only when the economics work.**
